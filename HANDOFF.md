# Watson's Flowers - handoff

A co-op delivery game for the family at Thanksgiving. The TV is the town
(Mesa and Gilbert, squeezed); every phone is a job at Watson's Flowers, the
shop Irene Watson started in 1927 selling roses door to door, and that
Grandpa Quist's family ran when he was a boy. One shared score: the day's
money.

Built from the plan agreed in conversation (2026-11-08): today's era, 1 to
20 players, anyone can take any job, tilt-to-steer driving, one combined
map. Burnout-style: fast, boosts, big crashes with flowers everywhere.

## The map

- **Real**: every mile road (Dobson to Greenfield; University to Warner) by
  its real name and position, names changing where they really do
  (Country Club becomes Arizona Ave at Guadalupe, Mesa Dr becomes McQueen
  and Stapley becomes Cooper at Baseline). US 60 and Loop 101 (straightened).
  The Tempe, Consolidated, Western and Eastern canals from OpenStreetMap.
  Landmarks by latitude/longitude: Watson's at 2525 W Main, Grandpa's at
  122 E Palo Verde St (its address comes out right from the numbering
  rule), the Mesa Temple, downtown Mesa with the Neon Garden (where the
  real sign stands now - there's a half-size copy), Mesa Arts Center, the
  Gilbert water tower and Heritage District, Freestone Park, the Riparian
  Preserve, MCC.
- **Squeezed 5x**: a mile is ~320 m. Street widths are not squeezed. The
  map is ~3.0 x 2.1 km. Watson's to Grandpa's is ~3.5 km by road, about a
  minute flat out.
- **Invented**: the half-mile collectors (named; one is Rose Lady Lane,
  one is Quist Lane), every neighbourhood (a loop street of stucco houses
  inside block walls), plazas with misters and ice chests, retention-basin
  parks (bowls you can jump out of), citrus groves, farms, desert lots.
- **Stunts**: a dirt ramp on the shoulder wherever a road crosses a canal
  squarely (82 of them) jumps the canal; black powder kegs on the canal
  banks (4 s of super-boost); Grandpa's crate on the Consolidated Canal
  nearest his house, once a day: a fireball, a water geyser, +$100.
- Re-fetch the OSM data with the Overpass query
  `way["waterway"="canal"](33.29,-111.91,33.43,-111.69); way["highway"~"^(motorway|trunk|primary|secondary)$"](...)`, `out tags geom;`
  (the maps.mail.ru mirror worked when overpass-api.de did not), save as
  `tools/osm/roads.json`, run `python3 tools/bake.py`.
- Google 3D tiles are not used (their terms have no family exception, and
  the shop is gone in their imagery anyway).

## The jobs (any phone, any job, change any time)

The computer covers whatever nobody holds - competent, never good, so one
more person always helps.

- **Driver** (up to 6 vans; the TV splits 1/2/2x2/3x2). Phone sideways like
  a steering wheel: tilt to steer (calibrated by "I'm holding it - GO" and
  CENTRE; angle of gravity across the screen, 2.5 deg dead zone, full lock
  at 32 deg, a soft curve). Left thumb BRAKE (dab it in a hard turn at speed
  to drift), right thumb GAS, BOOST top right, TOSS top left. "No tilting"
  gives finger steering; Auto-gas for small kids. Nobody driving: a
  computer van. A driver who goes quiet for 6 s: the computer drives until
  they're back.
- **Tosser**: rides with a driver. The phone shows the van from above and
  the porch; swipe towards it. Direction and strength both count (with a
  generous assist); speed adds a little error but a DRIVE-BY (over 22 m/s)
  pays x1.5, over 36 m/s x2. Perfect (within 1.3 m) x1.3, porch x1, lawn
  x0.6, missed = the order goes back to be remade. The driver's TOSS button
  auto-aims but gets sloppy with speed - a tosser is better.
- **Arranger**: claims an order card, taps in the stems the card asks for,
  picks wrap and ribbon, DONE. Quality: -12% per wrong stem, -12% wrong
  wrap or ribbon, +12% if done inside 12 s. Stems come out of the shared
  cooler. Walk-ins (green cards) appear whenever there are arrangers and pay
  straight away, so a room full of arrangers always has work.
- **Grower**: Irene's garden, 12 plots. Water dries out faster as it gets
  hotter; a plant in bloom cut = +5 stems. Without growers a wholesale truck
  restocks steadily; with them it slows to a trickle.
- **Dispatcher**: the whole map. Tap a van then an order: ready orders are
  saved for that van, ones already aboard become its next stop. Hold to
  ping a spot (a red beam on the TV); Goon alert.
- **Rose cart**: a little game for the youngest - drag Irene's cart along
  1927 Main Street, tap people with a heart to sell a rose ($2 to the team).
  Goons knock the roses over after 3 p.m.

## The day (7 minutes, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.)

- Orders are paced to the number of vans; a rush every 70-120 s.
  Customers wait 230 s, then cancel (reputation down).
- Heat: 72 F at 8, 117 F at 3:30. Bouquets in a van wilt faster as it
  heats up; tips scale with freshness. Patio misters perk them up, ice
  chests reset them.
- 3 p.m.: the Gilbert Goons on e-scooters chase loaded vans; at walking
  speed they grab a bouquet and flee. Hit a goon (over 5 m/s) and it flies,
  +$5, boost, and you get the bouquet back. They go home at 6:36.
- 6 p.m.: the haboob. Brown fog closes in, gusts shove the vans.
- 8 p.m.: results (money, deliveries, crashes and the insurance bill,
  awards per person), then the **Gilbert Days Parade**: everybody swipes
  flowers from the Watson's float (a girl waving on top) to the crowd on
  Gilbert Road. Then back to the shop.
- Holidays (picked in the lobby from any phone): Thanksgiving Eve, A Day on
  Main Street, Valentine's, Mother's Day - different recipes. Family record
  per holiday in the TV's localStorage (`wats_save`).

## Driving numbers (simcheck prints them)

0-60 mph ~1.9 s, top ~97 mph, boost ~104+ mph (black powder 165). A quarter
turn at 30 mph under 1 s. Crash: more than 16 m/s into something solid or
13 m/s closing on a car. Crashes lose half the cargo (all of it in a canal)
and the orders go back to be remade; 2.8 s of tumbling crash-cam, then the
van reappears on the nearest road, ghosted for 2.2 s. Boost fills from
drifts, near misses (within ~1.5 m of a car at 15+ m/s closing), air time,
smashing bins and bushes, goons.

Traffic spawns around the vans (about 22 per van, never within 85 m),
follows lanes, stops at the lights (16 s cycle at arterial crossings), and
brakes for vans in its lane.

## Protocol (`PROTO` 1)

Phone -> TV: `hello {name, dev, proto}`, `pick {role, ride|van}`,
`in {st, gas, brk, bst}` (driver, on change and every 100 ms), `act {a: {...}}`
(claim/stem/wrap/ribbon/done/drop, water/harvest, assign/prio/ping, rose,
toss, ptoss), `start {hol}`, `hol {k}`, `next`, `pause {v}`, `name`, `ping`.
TV -> phone: `host`, `you {role, van, id, drv, vans, ph}`, `s` (snapshot,
10 Hz, `WSim.snap`), `v` (a driver's/tosser's van, `WSim.vanInfo`), `fx`
(vibration), `full`. Order rows: `[id, walk, state, recipe, house, fresh,
van, by, prio, secsLeft, customer, pref, stems, wrap, ribbon]`.

## Things learned building it

- The van keeps its velocity in the old frame and only the nose turns;
  grip pulls the velocity round. Rotating the velocity with the nose (the
  first version) meant drifts never slid and never filled boost.
- The computer driver must keep right (a lane offset on its look-ahead
  point) - on the centre line it deadlocked with oncoming traffic that had
  stopped for it.
- Headless Chromium renders at ~5 fps in software, so the local join
  timeout is 8 s and netcheck's waits are long.

## Not built yet / not tried

- **Not tried on real phones or a real TV.** Tilt steering is the big
  unknown: tune `full` (32 deg) and the curve in `tiltSteer()` after the
  first family drive. iPhones need the motion permission tap (the GO button
  asks).
- **No repo/deploy yet**: needs a GitHub repo and a Cloudflare Worker
  pointed at it like the other games. The relay (TURN) needs the same two
  secrets as Mission Control.
- Six split screens on a slow laptop: untested for frame rate. Houses and
  trees are instanced (a few dozen draw calls per view); if it struggles,
  drop `renderer.setPixelRatio` to 1 or shorten the fog.
- Music is a small synthesized loop, not the recorded-instrument pipeline
  the other games use (`tools/music.py` from Mission Control would port).
- No voice lines, no crash replays (the crash cam is live).
